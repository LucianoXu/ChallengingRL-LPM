\begin{abstract}
\noindent We compare five exploration mechanisms---Learning Progress Monitoring (LPM), Random
Network Distillation (RND), the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), a raw next-state prediction-error
bonus (MSE), and a no-intrinsic A2C baseline (\textsc{none})---in the Miniworld 3D maze of Hou, An \&
Du (2026), \emph{Beyond Noisy-TVs: Noise-Robust Exploration via Learning Progress Monitoring}. The
maze emits \emph{no extrinsic reward}, so a method's quality is its \emph{spatial coverage}, and the
noisy-TV failure mode is geometric: a random-RGB ``noise wall'' at $z\approx 8$ separates the two
lower spawn rooms from the far room (room4), so a prediction-error agent fascinated by the wall
never crosses it. Two questions drive the design: \textbf{(RQ1)} how much coverage---and in
particular coverage \emph{past the wall}---does each method achieve under the clean vs.\ noisy
variants, and \textbf{(RQ2)} how does the \emph{spatial distribution} of exploration \emph{evolve
over training time}? RQ2 is answered by a new statistic we add to the LPM codebase: a
coverage-heatmap-evolution figure. All methods share one A2C base so that observed differences are
attributable to the exploration mechanism rather than the base learner. In a $30$-run downscaled
study we find one clean reproduction---under action-triggered noise, the raw-pixel method fixates on
the noisy TV $99\%$ of the time while LPM avoids it most ($2\%$)---alongside otherwise
variance-dominated coverage at this budget; the heatmap-evolution statistic renders the spatial
exploration dynamics clearly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and research questions}
The course topic is the exploration--exploitation trade-off. Deep RL drives directed exploration
with intrinsic-motivation bonuses (prediction error, novelty, learning progress). The \emph{noisy-TV}
problem is the canonical failure mode of prediction-error exploration: an unlearnable source of
randomness produces perpetually high prediction error, so a curiosity-driven agent fixates on it.
LPM rewards the \emph{improvement} of its dynamics model rather than the error level, and is
therefore noise-robust by construction. The 3D maze makes this failure spatial and directly
visualisable: the noise wall is a physical place the agent can get stuck at.

The maze geometry is a hand-designed four-room layout in an $18\times 12$ world (continuous units):
two spawn rooms below the wall (room1 $x\!\in\![0,4]$, room2 $x\!\in\![14,18]$, both $z\!\in\![0,8]$),
the thin grass-textured \emph{noise wall} (room3, $z\!\in\![8,8.1]$), and the large far room
(room4, $z\!\in\![8.1,12]$). The agent spawns in room1 and must traverse the wall to explore room4.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=nextline]
  \item[RQ1 (coverage \& noise robustness, reproduction).] Under the noisy variants
  (\texttt{noisy\_tv}: random-RGB wall $+$ 25\% sticky actions; \texttt{action\_noise}: an action
  that shows a random CIFAR-10 image) vs.\ the clean control (\texttt{nonoise}), which methods keep
  expanding coverage and which stall? How large is LPM's advantage in coverage \emph{past the wall}
  over the prediction-error methods (MSE, ICM, RND)?
  \item[RQ2 (spatial dynamics, novelty).] How does the spatial distribution of exploration evolve
  over training? Do prediction-error methods concentrate occupancy at the noise wall over time while
  LPM's coverage frontier advances into room4? We answer this with a coverage-heatmap-evolution
  figure (rows: method; columns: training-time window).
\end{description}

\section{Methods}
All five methods use the same A2C base (RMSprop, GAE with $\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$, a
single-epoch update every 64 steps, a Nature-style CNN over $160\times120$ RGB frames). Because the
maze extrinsic reward is identically zero, the per-step training reward reduces to a normalised
intrinsic term,
\begin{equation}
  r_t \;=\; \lambda_i \,\widehat{r}^{\,i}_t, \qquad
  \widehat{r}^{\,i}_t \;=\; \frac{r^{\,i}_t - \mu_t}{\sigma_t + \epsilon},
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_i = 0.1$ and $(\mu_t,\sigma_t)$ a running mean/std of the raw intrinsic reward. This
uniform running-std normalisation (RND-style) replaces the upstream notebooks' inconsistent, hand-set
offsets so that a single $\lambda_i$ is comparable across methods; for \textsc{none}, $r_t\equiv 0$
and the agent is a pure entropy-regularised random walker.

\section{Experimental design}
A single manipulation grid: method $\times$ variant $\times$ seed.

\paragraph{Grid.} Five methods $\times$ three variants (\texttt{nonoise}, \texttt{noisy\_tv},
\texttt{action\_noise}) $\times$ two seeds $=30$ runs. Each run is a single environment trained for
$2\times10^{4}$ steps (downscaled from the upstream $5\times10^{4}$ for local compute).

\paragraph{Budget.} $30$ runs $\times \sim\!4$\,min (decoder methods) on Apple-Silicon CPU
$\approx 1.5$\,h. Runs use CPU rather than MPS: the decoder methods (LPM, MSE) are \emph{faster} on
CPU because \texttt{ConvTranspose2d} falls back off the MPS device. The grid is resumable (completed
CSVs are skipped).

\section{Metrics}
Coverage is measured on the maze's native $72\times48$ occupancy grid (cells of $0.25$ world units),
restricted to the \emph{reachable} cells (the union of the four rooms; the empty corridor between the
spawn rooms is excluded). Let the final value of a quantity be its mean over the last $10\%$ of
logged updates, reported as mean$\,\pm\,$std over seeds.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{RQ1 headline --- beyond-wall coverage:}
  $\cov_{\text{bw}} = (\text{visited reachable cells with } z\ge 8.1)/(\text{reachable cells with } z\ge 8.1)$,
  i.e.\ the fraction of room4 explored. Prediction: under noise, MSE/ICM/RND collapse while LPM
  stays high.
  \item \textbf{Total coverage:} $\cov = (\text{visited reachable cells})/(\text{reachable cells})$,
  reported as a learning curve vs.\ frames.
  \item \textbf{Time-at-wall:} fraction of steps with $z\in[7.5,8.5]$---a direct measure of
  ``fascination with the noise.'' Prediction: highest for the pixel prediction-error methods under
  \texttt{noisy\_tv}.
  \item \textbf{RQ2 headline --- coverage-heatmap evolution (new statistic):} for each variant, a
  rows(method)$\times$columns(training-window) grid of top-down occupancy heatmaps, in two views:
  \emph{occupancy density} (log step-count per cell, revealing where the agent dwells) and
  \emph{coverage frontier} (cells ever visited up to each window, revealing how the explored region
  grows). The maze rooms and the $z=8$ wall line are overlaid for orientation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results (downscaled run)}
We ran the full $5\times3\times2 = 30$-run grid at $2\times10^{4}$ steps on CPU
($\approx$30\,min wall-clock). The results are \emph{qualitative}: at this budget the
between-seed variance is large, so they should be read as trends, not significance tests.

\paragraph{The clean positive: noisy-TV fixation under \texttt{action\_noise}.} The sharpest
result is the textbook noisy-TV trap. In the \texttt{action\_noise} variant, action~4 replaces the
observation with a random CIFAR-10 image. The raw-pixel predictor (MSE) is captured by it almost
completely---it selects action~4 on $98.6\%\pm1.4\%$ of steps and visits only $\sim\!10$ of the
$\sim\!1500$ reachable cells---while LPM selects it on just $2.0\%\pm1.7\%$ of steps, the
\emph{lowest} of all methods and far below the $20\%$ uniform rate (Fig.~\ref{fig:tv}). LPM is
noise-robust exactly as predicted: it does not reward the unlearnable image because its dynamics
model cannot \emph{improve} on it. ICM ($15\%$) and the \textsc{none} baseline ($7\%$) sit between;
RND is bimodal across seeds ($22\%\pm27\%$).

\paragraph{Coverage and beyond-the-wall.} The overall coverage ranking (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}) does
\emph{not} cleanly reproduce the paper's ``LPM dominates under noise'' headline. The no-intrinsic
\textsc{none} baseline is competitive everywhere, indicating that at $2\times10^{4}$ steps with a
single environment and entropy coefficient $0.05$, exploration is still largely driven by the policy's
own stochasticity rather than the intrinsic signal. LPM is slow to start (its coverage frontier only
advances in the later training windows) and ends mid-pack; MSE actually does well in the
pixel-wall \texttt{noisy\_tv} variant (the wall noise is a \emph{place} it can move past, not an
action that arrests it) yet collapses under \texttt{action\_noise} as above; RND collapses on the
clean \texttt{nonoise} variant (one seed never leaves the spawn room). These are budget- and
variance-dominated outcomes, not contradictions of the method---see Limitations.

\paragraph{The new statistic works.} The coverage-heatmap-evolution figure
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heat}) makes the spatial story legible: ICM and MSE spread across and past the wall
over successive windows, the \textsc{none} walk diffuses outward, and LPM's occupancy stays sparse
until late. It is the most informative single view of \emph{where} and \emph{when} each method
explores, and is the intended contribution of this experiment.

\section{Infrastructure and reproducibility}
The upstream maze experiment exists only as three Jupyter notebooks with an embedded A2C and no CLI.
We extracted the A2C engine, the LPM prediction/uncertainty nets, and the pixel decoder
\emph{verbatim} into an importable, CLI-driven package
(\texttt{LPM\_exploration/Miniworld/experiments/}), added canonical ICM and RND on the shared CNN
encoder, and added a per-step position log. The environment geometry is reused from our own
\texttt{miniworld\_play/envs.py} (a faithful port of the upstream maze). Each run writes a per-update
CSV (\texttt{update, step, frames, fps, visited\_count, coverage\_frac, beyond\_wall\_frac,
time\_at\_wall\_frac, int\_rew\_mean,} plus model/policy losses) and a compact \texttt{.npz} of
$(\text{step}, x, z, \text{action}, \text{sticky})$. A grid runner launches all runs sequentially
(resumable); an analysis script aggregates the CSVs and position logs into the coverage table, the
coverage curves, the beyond-wall and time-at-wall figures, and the heatmap-evolution figures. All
deviations are logged in \texttt{LPM\_exploration/UPSTREAM.md}.

\section{Limitations (stated up front)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Short budget, small sample.} $2\times10^{4}$ steps $\times\,2$ seeds, single
  environment, yields \emph{qualitative trends}, not rigorous statistical significance; several
  metrics have between-seed std comparable to their mean. A faithful reproduction needs the upstream
  $5\times10^{4}$ steps with many more seeds (and ideally vectorised environments on a cluster).
  \item \textbf{Intrinsic signal does not yet dominate.} The no-intrinsic \textsc{none} baseline is
  competitive on coverage, so at this budget exploration is largely driven by policy entropy rather
  than the intrinsic reward---which is why the coverage ranking (unlike the action-fixation result)
  does not separate the methods. This is a scale limitation, not evidence against the methods.
  \item \textbf{Uniform reward normalisation is a design choice.} Running-std normalisation makes a
  single $\lambda_i$ comparable across methods but deviates from the upstream per-method offsets;
  within-method clean-vs-noisy comparisons are the most reliable read.
  \item \textbf{ICM/RND are our ports.} They are canonical implementations on the shared encoder, not
  upstream code; the LPM and MSE paths are the verbatim-extracted ones.
  \item \textbf{Coverage is a 2D projection.} Occupancy is binned on the floor plane; agent heading
  and the first-person view (what actually drives the pixel predictors) are not visualised here.
\end{enumerate}
